"These horses don't just eat grass. They redesign landscapes through three mechanisms no engineering project could replicate. First, each horse weighs between 600 and 800 lb. Their hooves hit the ground with 800 lb of pressure per square in, more impact than a jackhammer. On soil that had turned to concrete, that's the kind of force needed to crack the crust and let water back in. But unlike a machine, horses are selective. They walk around young plants and only pound bare soil. Second, GPS tracking later revealed these horses travel about 15 kilometers a day in fractal patterns. They graze hard in one spot for hours, then relocate miles away, then circle back. This creates a patchwork of short grass, tall grass, and bare ground. And each microhabitat supports different species of insects, birds, and small mammals. The horses aren't just grazing, they're designing a mosaic. Third, and this is the part that sounds the most ridiculous, horse dung contains viable seeds from more than 20 different plant species. The trip through the horse's digestive system actually improves germination rates by up to 300%. One gram of dung holds up to 7.4 viable seedlings. Double the dispersal rate of cattle and eight times the rate of sheep. Every pile of manure along a 15 km daily route is a prefertilized seed packet."
